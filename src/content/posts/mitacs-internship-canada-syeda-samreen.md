Every year a handful of Pakistani students spend their summer in a Canadian research lab on a Mitacs Globalink Research Internship, fully funded: flights, stipend, the lot. In 2026 one of them was from our institute. Syeda Samreen Abid, a BS Geoinformatics student of the 2023 to 2027 batch at the Institute of Geo-Information and Earth Observation, spent twelve weeks at the University of Saskatchewan in Saskatoon, working on wildfire research with satellite data and presenting her results at the university's symposium.

We asked her to walk us through it, from the first time she heard about the programme to the advice she would give a first-year sitting in the same lab she sat in. Her answers are below, lightly edited.

> Participating in a fully funded research internship in Canada has been an exceptionally valuable experience for me. Working at the University of Saskatchewan gave me the chance to apply advanced GIS and remote sensing concepts to real-world projects while gaining international research exposure.

## Why she applied

Samreen did not stumble into it. She had wanted international exposure early in her degree, and when a senior from the university shared the Mitacs call, she treated it as a decision already made.

> Deep down, I always wanted to gain international exposure early in my academic years. It was not a sudden decision. When I got to know about Mitacs, I decided to apply without thinking about whether I would get accepted or not.

That last sentence is worth reading twice. The most common reason IGEO students do not get these internships is that they never apply.

## How the process actually worked

The [Mitacs Globalink Research Internship](https://www.mitacs.ca/our-programs/globalink-research-internship-students/) is open to undergraduates from Pakistan with at least one or two semesters left after the internship. You apply once, rank the projects you are interested in, and professors in Canada pick from the pool. Samreen's timeline:

- **September 2025.** Applied through the official Mitacs portal. The application asks for transcripts, a CV, references and your project choices, so start collecting those before the portal opens.
- **Autumn 2025.** Screening, project matching and interviews with prospective supervisors. Several rounds, not one.
- **February 2026.** Official acceptance. Then the visa, the flights and the accommodation, all of which she arranged herself.
- **Summer 2026.** Twelve weeks in Saskatoon.

> The selection procedure was thorough, involving multiple stages of application screening, project matching, and interviews. After completing all evaluation requirements, I received my official acceptance in February 2026.

Five months from application to answer, then another three to departure. If you are a second- or third-year student reading this in November, the next call is the one you should be preparing for now.

## What surprised her

Not the facilities, not the weather. The people.

> What impressed me most was how exceptionally supportive the faculty was, especially my supervisor. He ensured I made the most of this opportunity by encouraging me to attend valuable academic conferences. I also had the privilege of presenting my research on wildfires across Canada at the university symposium under his guidance.

Presenting your own work to a room of researchers as an undergraduate is not something most of us get to do before a final-year defence. It happened because her supervisor pushed for it, and because she said yes.

## What prepared her, and what she is proudest of

The technical side, she says, was already covered by the time she left Rawalpindi. The courses that mattered were the ones many students treat as side subjects.

> My coursework at Arid University in computer programming, GIS workflows, R scripting and Google Earth Engine established a strong technical foundation.

The wildfire work ran in Google Earth Engine, which meant writing code every day, exporting land-cover and night-light layers, and keeping track of what each script did. If you have been putting off the programming and scripting courses because they feel far from "real GIS", this is what real GIS looks like in a research lab abroad.

What she is proudest of is not a map.

> I am most proud of handling everything entirely on my own, from applying for this opportunity and the visa to booking my tickets and settling into life for three months in a completely different country. Learning so many new technologies during my time there, which I fully aim to integrate into my future research and career, was a major highlight.

## What she brought back

Three things, reading between her answers.

**Confidence that holds up abroad.** The skills taught at IGEO were enough to contribute in a Canadian research group from the first week. That is useful to know before you apply anywhere.

**A research record.** A summer of wildfire mapping, a symposium presentation and a supervisor who knows her work. That is what a strong graduate school application or a reference letter is built on.

**Independence.** Visa, flights, housing, a new city, a new lab, alone, at undergraduate age. Nothing in a classroom teaches that.

## Her advice to IGEO students

> Have complete confidence in your academic capabilities, and apply to every opportunity you see, because you never know when what will work out for you.

> Focus on developing strong practical skills in programming, spatial data tools and analytical methods. Be persistent throughout application processes. Dedication and preparation open doors to life-changing career milestones.

## If you want to do this next year

- **Start early.** The Mitacs portal typically opens in the late summer for the following year's internships. Have your transcript, CV and two referees ready before it does.
- **Build the skills she named.** Our [Remote Sensing Analyst](/roadmaps) and [GIS Developer](/roadmaps) roadmaps cover Python, R and Earth Engine with free resources at every stage.
- **Ask someone who has done it.** Samreen heard about Mitacs from a senior. Post your questions in the [forum](/forum) or look up alumni on the [directory](/alumni?skill=Google%20Earth%20Engine) who list Earth Engine among their skills.
- **Read the listing.** Mitacs Globalink now has its own page under [Opportunities](/opportunities/mitacs-globalink-research-internship), with eligibility, funding and the application timeline.

---
title: Precision Agriculture and Agri-tech
summary: Applying GIS, remote sensing and sensors to farm management. The track that most directly builds on the university's agricultural strengths.
order: 5
demand: Growing
tools: [Google Earth Engine, QGIS, Python, Drones, IoT sensors]
stages:
  - name: Foundations
    duration: Semester 1 to 3
    skills:
      - Crop calendars of Punjab and KP, soil and irrigation basics
      - Vegetation indices and what they actually measure
      - Field data collection with QField or ODK
    resources:
      - label: QField documentation
        url: https://docs.qfield.org/
  - name: Monitoring
    duration: Semester 3 to 5
    skills:
      - Crop type and acreage mapping with Sentinel-2 in Earth Engine
      - Yield estimation, stress detection, irrigation scheduling
      - "Weather and climate data: CHIRPS, ERA5, PMD stations"
    resources:
      - label: Earth Engine agriculture tutorials
        url: https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/tutorials
  - name: Farm scale
    duration: Semester 5 to 7
    skills:
      - Multispectral drone surveys and prescription maps
      - Variable rate application concepts
      - Soil sampling design and interpolation
      - "Working with farmers: communication and trust"
  - name: Job ready
    duration: Final year
    skills:
      - A season-long monitoring project on a real farm or research plot
      - Apply to agri-tech startups, seed and fertiliser companies, FAO, CABI, provincial agriculture departments, and the university's own research groups
---

Few GIS graduates specialise here, which is exactly why those who do are in demand. The university's agriculture faculties and research farms are an advantage no other GIS programme in the country has.
